Ngorongoro

A collapsed volcanic caldera cradling one of Africa's densest wildlife populations — a self-contained world, walled by its own rim, where the Big Five roam a single unbroken crater floor.

Best Time to Visit Year-Round
Ideal For Big Five & black rhino
Nearest Access 2 hrs from Arusha
Crater Floor 610m below the rim

The Landscape

A world sealed
inside its own walls

Some three million years ago a vast volcano collapsed in on itself, leaving behind the largest unbroken, unflooded volcanic caldera on Earth. Its rim rises over 600 meters above a floor of grassland, soda lake and acacia forest — a natural amphitheater for one of the richest ecosystems in Africa.

Because the walls are steep and the water permanent, an extraordinary density of wildlife stays resident year-round, including one of Tanzania's last strongholds of the critically endangered black rhino. This is also a UNESCO World Heritage Site, shared with the Maasai who still graze cattle on its rim.

"Nowhere else do you drive down into the wild rather than out to meet it — the crater simply holds you inside it."

What Awaits

Highlights of Ngorongoro

01

The Crater Floor

Descend to a self-contained ecosystem of grassland, forest and soda lake, home to an estimated 25,000 large animals year-round.

02

Black Rhino Sightings

One of the most reliable places in Africa to see the critically endangered black rhino in its natural habitat.

03

Olduvai Gorge

Visit the "Cradle of Mankind," where some of the oldest hominid fossils on Earth were discovered, a short drive from the crater.

04

Empakaai Crater

A quieter, less-visited caldera holding a deep soda lake and flocks of flamingos, reached by a scenic descent on foot.

05

Maasai Communities

Meet the Maasai who continue to graze livestock within the Conservation Area under a rare shared-use arrangement.

06

Rim Forest Views

Montane forest along the crater rim offers cool air, elephant sightings and sweeping views down onto the floor below.

Practical Notes

Plan Your Visit

Best Time to Go

  • Year-round wildlife thanks to the crater's permanent water
  • June–October for the clearest skies and easiest driving
  • January–February for a chance at cooler, quieter game drives

Getting There

  • 2-hour drive from Arusha to the crater rim
  • Often combined with the Serengeti as part of a circuit
  • Steep descent road down to the crater floor by 4x4 only

What to Pack

  • A warm layer — the rim sits above 2,200m and gets cold
  • Sturdy shoes for the Empakaai crater descent on foot
  • Binoculars for distant rhino and cat sightings on the floor

Suggested Journey

The Ngorongoro Crater Explorer

Three days centered on the caldera — a full day descending to the crater floor in search of black rhino and resident lion, a visit to Olduvai Gorge, and a rim-side lodge with views straight down into the wild below.
