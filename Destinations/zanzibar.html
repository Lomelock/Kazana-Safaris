The Spice Island

Where the trade winds
still set the tide

For centuries, Zanzibar sat at the crossing point of Arab, Indian and African trade routes, and the island still carries that layered history in its coral-stone architecture, carved doors and the scent of clove drying in the sun. Stone Town’s alleys open, without warning, onto the sea.

Beyond the old town, the coastline unfolds into some of the palest, warmest water in the Indian Ocean — coral gardens just offshore, sandbanks that rise and vanish with the tide, and dhow sails catching the last light of the day.

"Half the island smells of cloves, half of it smells of salt — Zanzibar never quite decides which sea to belong to."

What Awaits

Highlights of Zanzibar

Urithi — heritage

Stone Town

A UNESCO World Heritage old town of coral-stone houses, carved doors and rooftop views over the harbour.

Karafuu — clove

Spice Farms

Walk working plantations of clove, cardamom and vanilla, and taste the harvest straight from the tree.

Bahari — sea

Turquoise Reefs

Snorkel or dive coral gardens just offshore, in some of the clearest, warmest water in the Indian Ocean.

Jahazi — dhow

Sunset Dhow Cruises

Sail a traditional wooden dhow at golden hour, with fresh-caught seafood grilled on deck.

Kobe — tortoise

Prison Island

Meet giant Aldabra tortoises, some over a century old, on a short boat crossing from Stone Town.

Pwani — shore

East Coast Beaches

Paje and Bwejuu's powder-white sand and shallow lagoons, framed by swaying palms and low tide sandbars.

Practical Notes

Plan Your Visit

Best Time to Go

  • June–October for dry, breezy days and calm seas
  • December–February for warm water and quiet beaches
  • Avoid the long rains of April–May for reef visibility

Getting There

  • Direct flights from Arusha or a short charter connection from the northern safari circuit
  • International arrivals land at Abeid Amani Karume International Airport
  • Easy to pair with a mainland safari for a classic Tanzania combination

What to Pack

  • Reef-safe sunscreen and a light cover-up for Stone Town
  • Snorkel gear, or hire it locally at the reef sites
  • Modest clothing for visiting mosques and old-town streets
