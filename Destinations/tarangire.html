Tarangire

Ancient baobabs and some of the largest elephant herds in East Africa, gathered along a river that never runs dry — a quieter, more intimate alternative to the busier northern circuit.

Best Time to Visit June – October
Ideal For Elephants & baobabs
Nearest Access 1.5–2 hrs from Arusha
Park Size 2,850 km²

The Landscape

Where the river
never runs dry

Named for the Tarangire River that cuts through its heart, the park becomes a lifeline during the dry season, when many of the region’s large mammals gather around its permanent water. Elephant herds of 200 or more are common here, and the concentration of wildlife is especially striking in the drier months.

The landscape is unmistakable: swollen baobabs, some more than a thousand years old, rise from golden grasslands dotted with termite mounds. Fewer visitors make it this far north of Arusha, which adds to the feeling of space, stillness and true wilderness.

"You come for the elephants and stay for the baobabs — trees that were already ancient when this land was still unmapped."

Highlights of Tarangire

01

Great Elephant Herds

Witness some of the largest elephant congregations in Tanzania, especially along the riverbanks during the dry season.

02

Ancient Baobabs

Iconic, swollen-trunked baobab trees — some over a thousand years old — punctuate the landscape at every turn.

03

Silale Swamp

A permanent wetland drawing elephant, buffalo and predators alike, especially spectacular in the dry months.

04

The Tarangire River

The park's lifeline, sustaining wildlife through the harshest months when other water sources disappear.

05

Exceptional Birdlife

Over 550 recorded species make Tarangire one of the finest birding destinations in East Africa.

06

Fewer Crowds

A quieter alternative to the northern circuit's busier parks, with vast tracts of wilderness largely to yourself.

Practical Notes

Plan Your Visit

Best Time to Go

  • June–October for peak elephant concentrations along the river
  • November–May for lush scenery and prolific birdlife
  • Quieter year-round than the Serengeti or Ngorongoro

Getting There

  • 1.5–2 hour drive from Arusha by private 4x4
  • Often paired with Lake Manyara as a short circuit
  • Charter flights available to the Kuro airstrip

What to Pack

  • Binoculars for birding and distant elephant herds
  • Neutral clothing for walking safaris where permitted
  • Sun protection — shade is limited on the open plains
